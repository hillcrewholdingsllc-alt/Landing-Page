What Pitt County says happens when taxes become delinquent

Pitt County states that its Tax Collector may use collection remedies authorized by North Carolina law, including attachments, garnishments, debt setoff and tax foreclosure on real estate. If unpaid taxes are referred for foreclosure, legal fees and court costs can increase the amount required to resolve the matter.

The exact status of your account matters. A delinquent bill, a collection notice, a filed foreclosure action and a scheduled sale are very different stages, so deadlines should be verified directly with Pitt County and, when appropriate, an attorney.

If you are considering a sale, gather:
  • The most recent tax bill or delinquency notice.
  • Any foreclosure complaint, attorney letter or sale notice.
  • Mortgage payoff information if there is a loan.
  • Other liens or judgments you know about.
  • The property address, condition and occupancy.

Can delinquent taxes be handled through a real estate closing?

Often, taxes and other valid liens are reviewed during title work and paid or otherwise resolved as part of closing when sufficient proceeds and legal authority exist. The closing attorney determines what must be satisfied for marketable title and how funds are disbursed.

North Carolina law provides for payment of tax liens from proceeds in certain judicial and power-of-sale transactions, but every private sale depends on the actual title, payoff amounts and closing requirements.

Do not rely on a generic foreclosure deadline

Tax foreclosure timing depends on the specific case. Pitt County explains that referred cases may proceed through a filed legal action, public auction and an upset-bid process. If you have received a legal notice, use the date and contact information on that notice rather than assuming a website timeline applies to your situation.

Official local information is available from Pitt County Delinquent Accounts & Foreclosure Auctions.
